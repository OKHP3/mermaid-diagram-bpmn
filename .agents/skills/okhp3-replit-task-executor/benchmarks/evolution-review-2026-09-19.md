# Three-pass evolution review

Evaluated package: `okhp3-replit-task-executor`, v0.1.0 baseline to v1.2.0
release, with an interim v0.2.0 development candidate. Decision scope: a supervised, portable handoff-and-return contract.
This is structural and analytical evidence. It is not a live production or
cross-host benchmark, an unseen holdout, or a measured cost comparison.

The owner requested exactly three Foundry and Equilibrium improvement passes.
Each pass freezes its artifact and acceptance criteria, separates evidence,
outcome, and safety/portability reviewers, uses conditional falsification, and
records an evidence-led adjudication. Separate reviewer contexts share a model
family and source packet; agreement is correlated evidence.

The target outcome retains Replit as PM, planner, and designer while external
agents own architecture, code, technical validation, and protected integration.
Private board bodies, task links, screenshots, transcripts, and machine paths
are excluded from the distributed records. Development scenarios are synthetic.

## Predeclared acceptance

- Preserve the owner's role split and original criteria, including applicable
  design acceptance and revision routing.
- Preserve existing authority and ownership protection without repeated
  permission requests or blanket cancellation.
- Distinguish context, configuration, provenance, and native board control.
- Bind completion evidence to task identity and immutable revisions; expose
  pending design, board, dependency, source, and deployment outcomes separately.
- Gate administrative return on actual capabilities, effects, authority, and
  usage, including races, queue wakeups, and stale Apply operations.
- Retain honest maturity, historical evidence, and unmeasured performance limits.
- Validate paths/resources and exact mirror content. No unresolved critical
  authorization, data-loss, collision, or false-completion defect is acceptable.

## Evidence interpretation

The earlier v0.1.0 exercise recovered source and preserved a backlog. It did
not demonstrate the complete external execution and native completion cycle.
The post-creation observations in `references/replit-semantics.md` motivate
changes but do not certify current-version behavior. All packaged evaluation
cases are visible development cases. Release holdout status is
`external-required`; `holdout_seen` is true.

All three passes are complete. The final decision is approve-with-limits for
a supervised draftable contract. Version 1.2.0 was selected at the owner's request;
the increment does not claim a live pilot, measured savings, or higher maturity.

## Pass 1: close the observed contract gaps

Three separate-context reviewers agreed on the required role, mechanism, and
bounded-return corrections. Conditional falsification identified an
administrative submission race and stale rendered design evidence. Both became
development cases and explicit guards. The negotiator analytically exercised
the counterexamples and approved advancement with limits. No live action was
performed. See `benchmarks/pass-1-review.json`.

## Pass 2: test the contract and challenge integration

Three independent reviewers manually traced 21 development cases and 63
expectations. They found no material defect in those visible cases, but noted
ambiguous hash ordering. Conditional disruption found a new target-base race:
a reviewed PR head can remain unchanged while the combined merge result breaks.
An isolated local Git fixture reproduced a clean merge with a failing criterion.
The fix requires combined-result validation and supported protection against both
head and relevant base movement, or verified serialization. A 22nd case records
this regression. The negotiator approved advancement after retesting the fix.
The private-receipt counterexample failed against existing privacy rules.
See `benchmarks/pass-2-review.json`; this remains analytical contract evidence.

## Pass 3: retain the corrected contract and bind the release

The three role reviews traced all 22 visible cases and 66 expectations, plus
20 visible routing examples. Every expected decision had instruction support.
Conditional disruption tested implicit publication and dirty-tree test
attribution; both conflicted with existing authority and evidence rules. The
negotiator retained the contract without speculative additions.

The owner requested v1.2 during this pass. Exact normalized-byte comparison
confirmed that the final v1.2.0 core differs from the frozen v0.2.0 candidate only
in version labels; the negotiator independently bound the review to that release.
See `pass-3-review.json` and `pass-3-development-review.json`. Historical pass
records retain the versions and hashes they actually examined.

Separate contexts share a model family and source packet. The final safety
review reused the previous adjudicator context after a fresh-thread capacity
limit, without reading its sibling reviews. This additional correlation is a
review limit. All examples were visible; these counts are analytical coverage,
not measured executor success rates.

## Release and future evidence

Retain one portable package at v1.2.0, synchronized to both authorized
repositories. Validate its structure, JSON, linked resources, finite evaluation
inventory, privacy boundary, catalog metadata, and complete mirror bytes.
`sync-manifest.json` defines the guarded transfer and exclusions. The reviewed
core hash excludes `benchmarks/` to avoid a self-referential digest; full mirror
verification includes the evidence files.

Maturity remains draftable. The next stronger evidence would be one authorized
end-to-end native return with actual effects observed, followed by cross-host
checks, an external unseen holdout, and matched cost/quality measurements.
The owner or next executor owns that trial. Re-review when the contract,
platform capabilities, side effects, ownership controls, or evidence changes.
